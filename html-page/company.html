What is ARENA

Every business has different ambitions, whether raising capital to grow, providing liquidity for shareholders or preparing for a future IPO. ARENA and Nasdaq Dubai are complementary platforms within DFM’s broader capital markets offering. ARENA is DFM’s planned regulated private market platform. Three authorities, one streamlined framework:

  • Capital Market Authority (CMA) — the UAE federal securities regulator (formerly SCA). Sets the offering rules, approves prospectuses, sets disclosure standards and licenses brokers and advisers.
  • Dubai Financial Market (DFM) — sets the ARENA listing rules and supervises the market.
  • Ministry of Economy and Free Zones — the company registrar. Sets the corporate governance code.

ARENA is expected to launch soon, subject to final regulatory approvals. The CMA has completed its public consultation on ARENA’s offering rules, and preparations are progressing towards launch. Register your interest to receive updates and be among the first to know when ARENA opens.

Requirements are calibrated by route. For the Growth Market route:

  • Revenue generation track record of 2 financial years
  • AED 5M fully paid-up capital (US$200k for PLCs)
  • Shareholder equity at least 100% of paid-up capital (no accumulated losses)
  • Unqualified 1-year working capital statement
  • Shares unencumbered, freely transferable and with equal rights for owners
  • 25% market-mandated free float
  • Incorporated in the UAE (Private JSC) or its free zones (PLC)
  • Two years of financials fully audited by a CMA-licensed auditor
  • Incorporation documents (Articles of Association, MoA) and a board resolution to list
  • CMA-approved prospectus where a capital raise is involved

The regulator may grant exemptions from specific requirements on a case-by-case basis.

ARENA offers flexibility over investor participation depending on the chosen route. On the Private Market route, companies decide which investors can participate through a controlled, whitelisted investor group, giving greater control over their shareholder base and access to disclosures. The Growth Market route provides broader access to institutional and eligible retail investors. The Closed Listing route lets companies admit their shares without a public capital raise, with trading limited to approved shareholders. All routes remain subject to applicable regulatory and investor eligibility requirements.

The early look is a preliminary investor engagement process that assesses market appetite before an offering goes ahead. Companies engage with selected institutional and potential anchor investors to gather feedback on valuation, pricing and investment interest. This helps gauge indicative demand, identify potential cornerstone investors and inform the offering structure and pricing strategy before the final decision to proceed.

Both routes offer controlled trading through a Request for Quote (RFQ) mechanism and periodic auctions, rather than continuous trading. Transactions are restricted to approved, whitelisted investors, so companies can manage shareholder participation and facilitate secondary liquidity within a regulated market environment.

Growth Market and Private Market raises take 3–4 months:

  • Eligibility check (a few days): initial self assessment with key documents.
  • Application (5 days): full application form and documents; first meetings with financial advisers.
  • Transaction preparation (4–5 weeks): appoint a financial adviser, finalise the equity story and structure, and start the standardised offering prospectus template.
  • Early look investor engagement (1–2 weeks): test appetite and build the shadow book, ahead of the go-live decision.
  • Intention to float (1 week): float announcement via the platform, plus investor education and roadshow.
  • Bookbuilding (1 week): price range announced; institutional and retail investors subscribe via the platform and through brokers; final price and allocation announced.
  • Listing and secondary trading: shares are admitted to trading through existing brokers.
  • Ongoing disclosure and reporting.
